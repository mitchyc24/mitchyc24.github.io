How it works

      
One line. A slicer treats a snowflake as dozens of little islands and hops between them. Here the
        nozzle moves to the start once and then never stops extruding: the shape is filled with passes one line apart, and
        every pass is spliced into its neighbour, so each layer is a single closed loop that ends where the next one begins.
        Drag the slider under the preview to follow it.

      
Strong joins. Odd and even layers use the same passes but splice them in different places, so no
        join sits on top of another.

      
Printing it. The file is for a Snapmaker 2.0 A350 or A350T with the single-extruder toolhead and a
        0.4 mm nozzle. Its warm-up and finish follow Snapmaker's own printer profile. Copy it to a USB stick or send it
        from Luban, level the bed as usual, and watch the first layer go down before you leave it.

      
Locks. A locked property keeps its value when you randomize. Lock the type to stay in one family,
        or lock everything except the seed to get variations on one design (give it some irregularity first).
